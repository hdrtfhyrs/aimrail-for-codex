# Find a workable approach

When you need to increase the current search entry point's query budget, continue GitHub pagination, or retrieve the complete candidate set, use [Search coverage and continuation](references/search-coverage.md). Interface ordering does not replace the task judgments in this document. Run all `node` commands from the repository root; executable paths refer to the shared implementation.

When the cause of a failure, capability judgment, or retrospective is unclear, first read [Object identification and cause diagnosis](references/diagnosis.md). Place the observed symptom within the actual operation and its upstream and downstream steps before forming research questions. Handle small issues with known causes and fixes directly, without adding a diagnostic workflow.

First follow “Understand the current task” in the [shared rules](../../integrations/AGENTS.md). Connect the project, branch, complete message, and actual operation to form an initial understanding of the desired change, its purpose, and key unknowns. If a gap could change the goal or approach, investigate how others define and accomplish comparable work. Bring the concepts, dependencies, and counterexamples back to the user's words to revise assumptions, then form the complete current task and next research question. Question formation, initial search, revised understanding, close reading, and execution inform one another. Do not commit to a habitual solution and then search only for supporting material. External authors' goals help understanding; this project's user goal and applicable conditions still govern the work. Local conditions, installed tools, and old conclusions do not bound the candidates. Adapt domain knowledge and the user's assets to their actual uses.

For questions requiring selection, involving changing methods or effects, or having mature practices, investigate the task itself before deciding an approach, rather than only researching how the research tools work. Start with mature solutions producing comparable results, authors' implementations, and practitioners' methods, then examine user feedback, failures, and counterexamples. Adjust questions and candidates when the initial search reveals new leads. Read current official originals for product mechanisms; use evaluations, authors' papers/code, and original accounts of practice for effects and costs. For business work, examine demand, labor, and after-sales experiences for comparable deliverables. For technical work, examine implementations, issues, and reproductions in the same version and environment. For creative work, examine authors' methods and reader feedback in the relevant genre and form, then judge against this project's actual work. Choose sources by the question; a list of websites is not a mandatory search checklist.

When reading feedback, establish what the author actually did, when, with what object, task scale, tools/versions, investment, and result. Distinguish firsthand experience, retelling, promotion, speculation, and proposed fixes. One post can reveal a failure condition; it cannot establish general effectiveness, failure probability, or the user's benefit. Multiple reposts are not independent evidence. Resolve adoption-changing disputes through official sources, source code, or a necessary small sample. Distinguish vendor self-evaluations, independent evaluations, individual reports, and local measurements. Do not apply an old benchmark ranking to the current task. Add only evidence that can affect the current decision, without mechanically filling source or search quotas.

For broad discovery, first collect many titles, origins, and links using varied queries, Chinese and international terminology, and relevant sources chosen by the AI for the current task. Titles are both entry points and clues to different principles, implementations, resources, and practices. After seeing a broad set of titles, connect them to the user's goal to consider previously overlooked approaches, combinable conditions, and assumptions that may need revision. Make an initial AI judgment, then freely select items worth a description or original-source reading. Do not assert feasibility from titles during that initial judgment; establish it through original material and actual conditions. “Titles → scoring → fixed top few” is not a substitute for thinking. Do not prune at collection time by stars, search rank, hit frequency, or literal overlap. Retain new names, low-profile items, and titles not yet understood. Persist every lead; return compact titles first, expanding original material and detailed diagnostics as needed. Continue losslessly when the display window is too long; undisplayed material remains retrievable. Read selected originals or obtain more titles without requiring every catalog page to be read first or an item-by-item reading justification. Revise queries, understanding, and methods with discoveries, and act once there is enough evidence for the current decision. Set no fixed article count, mandatory reading count, or requirement to search until nothing new appears. Titles, descriptions, and demos do not replace evidence; follow the author's implementation and applicable conditions for key approaches. Do not mechanically turn a specific fact check about a known object or a small deterministic action into a large investigation.

When finding a feasible approach, understanding an unfamiliar object, choosing a route, correcting old assumptions, or handling an unresolved blocker, the current AI must take these actual actions without waiting for another reminder to search: form the missing question from the complete goal and applicable conditions; choose concrete queries capable of revealing different approaches; call the current `explore_search` with `goal` and a `queries` array (use `terms` separately for catalog terminology). The CLI is `node plugins/search-tools/cli.mjs explore --goal "Current purpose and question" --queries-file "Path to the AI-saved query-array JSON" --mode topic`. The AI chooses queries; do not automatically append recommendation/alternative/awesome to every query. The array is call data, not a form the user must fill out or a separate plan approval. Once the first search returns titles, connect them to the goal and make the initial judgment described above, then read selected sources, add queries, or retrieve further titles autonomously. If a newly read approach changes the current plan, bring it into execution. Use `topic` for comparable goals, `tools` for finding tools, and `obstacle` with `error` for blockers. The shared background `start` also accepts `--queries-file`; legacy `--terms` still accepts original terminology. Reading/editing this skill does not start a search: the current AI performs the call. The program handles batch execution, persistence, pagination, and return. `catalog.next` retrieves more titles; `search_next` continues the search. Retrieve complete diagnostics from persistent originals or with `diagnostics=true` as needed. Undisplayed, unexecuted, and low-literal-overlap items cannot be treated as irrelevant. Continue already started work for the same `goal` according to its state, avoiding duplicate parallel work overwriting it. Follow the search coverage reference for operations.

If results are irrelevant, change the question or entry point. Handle empty results, verification challenges, parsing failures, and network failures separately. When blocked, investigate both the error and how others achieve the same goal. Switch as results require among native search, Chinese engines, official APIs, site search, and a normally authenticated browser. A failure to read through one route does not mean the material does not exist. If tools are insufficient, investigate extensible implementations, plugins, and independent adapters. Establish the real interface, permissions, loading, and processing conditions, and implement a feasible route. Finding a plugin does not establish that it can modify native features. If only an approximation is possible, preserve the gap to the goal, unmet items, and nearest continuation. Read original sources for key conclusions. Report discovery, reading, and actual usability separately. Queries in Chinese do not establish that Chinese authors were read; researching only an existing route does not establish coverage of all candidates.

Search also builds understanding of objects. When encountering a specific object, relate its name to its category, structure, and purpose. Follow causal relationships to fill in a set of knowledge useful for judgment; when new material reveals neighboring concepts, counterexamples, or conditions, investigate their actual effect. For example, MoE active parameters concern computation, while total parameters and weight precision concern storage; these require separate understanding. Through `knowledge-recall`, return common knowledge to its category/concept and specific differences to the concrete object, so similar objects can later reuse it. The AI chooses depth around actual use and valuable neighboring knowledge; do not wait for tool errors to search or treat arbitrary encyclopedia expansion or search counts as the result.

Bring evidence back to the current task: explain what to adopt or decline, how applicable conditions differ, and how the evidence changes candidates, delivery scope, responsibilities, or verification methods, then continue research or execution. A source list does not replace that judgment. Check only assumptions whose failure would change the plan; make a small sample in a new directory when necessary. Actively seek counterexamples and alternative explanations, treating technical judgments from the user, coordinator, skills, and subagents equally. Check against user requirements, key originals, and actual results; reflection by the same model or a reviewer's agreement is not verification. Close the investigation when evidence supports the current action and remaining unknowns cannot change it, then add evidence if execution reveals something new. Do not call research complete when reading failed or evidence is insufficient, or add approval, scoring, or reading-signoff procedures. Use a separately manageable task window for independent research. Delegate as needed if it contains an independent material domain or key blind spot; the responsible executor synthesizes judgment and reads key outputs. Historical wording only helps locate material. Read [Relevant history](references/history.md) as needed; it cannot override the user's current intent. Adjust unsuitable methods, and maintain or remove content confirmed obsolete and useless.

In discussion, deliver an evidenced approach the user can act on: conclusion, key steps, necessary time/cost, and tradeoffs only the user can decide. Once work is assigned, keep implementing it without waiting for a fixed “execute” command. Briefly explain unfamiliar domains first; supplement an existing knowledge document only when a written explanation is needed. Present blockers as steps toward a solution. If the result really cannot be achieved, state the evidence and nearest feasible continuation. Do not let process audits or unrelated risk lists dominate the response.

When research actually changes the current method, update the active case through [knowledge-recall](../knowledge-recall/SKILL.md), preserving its trigger, applicable conditions, actual adoption, and unverified boundaries. Narrowly update the relevant skill for repeatable methods; turn deterministic operations into scripts as needed. Preserve key original evidence. Do not compress source recommendations, research reports, or one local success into a universal rule, or add a record on every invocation. If later execution or user corrections overturn an old action, update the same original, replace the active method, and retain recovery references. Successful storage and retrieval prove only that material is obtainable; later outcomes determine continued adoption.
